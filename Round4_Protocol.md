# Independent controller evaluation protocol

The rate target is 3.0 Mbit/s over 1 MHz with equal half-duplex phases. The corresponding per-hop Shannon threshold is 6 bit/s/Hz and about 18.0 dB SINR. The earlier 4.5-Mbit/s setting requires 9 bit/s/Hz and remains a high-load sensitivity case. A five-scene sanity run on development seeds 3000–3004 preceded the full campaign; no holdout result informed the target. The simulator continues to use the same continuous-rate model, interference generator, motion limits, and GP implementation.

Seeds 3000–3099 form a fresh development set. On these scenes, the service-only GP sampler is run at temperature 0.05, 0.1, or 0.3 and raw velocity spread 4 or 8 m/s. After these six development configurations, 2 m/s spread was added at temperatures 0.05 and 0.1 to examine the projection concern. This extension was made before any holdout result was inspected. The pair with the lowest development mean outage is chosen; an exact tie is broken by the lower 90th percentile of longest outage, then by the lower temperature and spread. The chosen pair is written to `results_round4/selection.dat` before any holdout result is inspected. The development set also tests the selected corridor variant and midpoint tracking to audit batch stability.

The graph-search settings are selected independently on the same development scenes, comparing beam width 16 and depth 6 with beam width 32 and depth 8. The same mean-outage and longest-outage selection rule applies, with the smaller graph used for any exact tie. Here “selection” refers to route performance, not calibration of outage probabilities.

Seeds 4000–4199 form the independent holdout. All controllers use the same generated field and user trajectory for each seed. The five prespecified paired comparisons are selected GP against midpoint tracking, GP-mean lattice search, reactive return, corridor reward, and parametric jammer fit. Two-sided paired sign-flip permutation tests use 20,000 deterministic draws; their p-values are Holm-adjusted across these five comparisons. The nominal sampler, known-field sampler, known-field lattice search, and two-stage schedule are diagnostic references. Confidence intervals use the scenario as the unit, never the slot.

The same holdout seeds are also evaluated at the former 4.5-Mbit/s target with the selected hyperparameters locked. GP sampler, midpoint tracking, GP-map lattice search, and known-field sampler are the four high-load policies. These results are a secondary operating-point check and do not enter the five-comparison 3.0-Mbit/s Holm family.

The lattice search follows the radio-map-to-graph idea of Zhang and Zhang (IEEE TWC 2021) but adapts it to a moving two-hop relay. It expands nine acceleration actions at each future slot, retains the selected number of lowest-cost partial paths, uses the GP mean in a two-hop risk score, and executes only the first velocity. It is a beam-search baseline, not a reproduction of that paper's fixed-endpoint shortest-path algorithm. Its known-field counterpart supplies the true interference field to the same reachable search. A second oracle enumerates all $9^3$ dynamically reachable action sequences over three slots using the true field and the actual future user positions. It is exact for this short action lattice, not a full-mission continuous optimum. All three graph controllers use the same speed and acceleration limits as the sampler.

All experiment results have `.dat` filenames. The third-round 4.5-Mbit/s data and 30-scene scans remain available as separate sensitivity and historical diagnostics. The reproducibility package contains the current code and supporting results.

Reviewer follow-up checks use the same single-file experiment entry point. `review-checks` and `robustness` use development seeds 3000–3029 and are exploratory; `risk-audit` retrospectively evaluates planning scores on the saved 200-scene holdout routes; `boundary-audit` counts realized-velocity changes after boundary clipping. These checks were added after the primary holdout analysis. They are not part of its five-test Holm family, and no new controller setting is selected from their outcomes.

The feedback follow-up reruns the selected GP policy on the 200 holdout seeds with two altered observation rules: no user radio reports and an ideal side channel delivering user reports even after outage. Both are compared scene by scene with the saved service-gated GP run. These rules were evaluated after the original holdout inspection, so their confidence intervals are descriptive and do not join the prespecified Holm family.

The boundary-safe sensitivity uses the same locked sampler temperature, velocity spread, and 3.0-Mbit/s target. It replaces boundary clipping in both candidate rollouts and executed motion by a velocity projection that constrains speed, acceleration, the next position, and a conservative per-axis braking distance. The available acceleration is divided by $\sqrt{2}$ for the two horizontal axes when computing the braking limit; the full $4\,\mathrm{m/s^2}$ norm limit is retained for the resulting velocity change. GP sampling and midpoint tracking are both rerun on development seeds 3000–3029. On the 200 original holdout seeds 4000–4199, the boundary-safe GP is rerun and compared with the original midpoint trajectories, whose separate 200-scene audit found zero realized acceleration violations. The midpoint results are identical on the 30 development seeds with and without the new projection. The rule was designed after the primary holdout analysis, so even the 200-scene comparison is retrospective sensitivity analysis rather than a new prespecified confirmation. Per-scene output and aggregate values are saved under `.dat` filenames.

After the numerical runs, short Chinese comments were added to seven source files. Their Python syntax trees were checked against the pre-annotation versions and are identical. The 3200 holdout, batch-audit, and high-load records retain their original run signature in `comment_only_previous_signature` and use the annotated-source signature for cache checks. The before/after source hashes and invariant numerical-payload hash are recorded in `results_round4_annotation_audit.dat`.
